This scenario is a **symmetric standoff**: two equal fleets with perfectly mirrored ship and weapon stats spawn on opposite sides of the map and advance toward a contested central band. I chose medium-long weapon range, solid but not excessive durability, and moderate mobility so neither side can instantly collapse the other, but both are dangerous once firing lines form. The result is a battle where spacing, target selection, and clean approach vectors matter more than raw stat asymmetry.

The central obstacle field is intentionally sparse-to-moderate rather than dense. That creates multiple firing lanes and intermittent line-of-sight breaks, which is useful for training because ships must alternate between holding formation, sliding around cover, and committing to brief pushes when targets are exposed. Since both factions are identical, any advantage comes from micro-behavior alone, making this a strong benchmark for learning disciplined engagement, focus fire, and collision-aware maneuvering under fair conditions.
