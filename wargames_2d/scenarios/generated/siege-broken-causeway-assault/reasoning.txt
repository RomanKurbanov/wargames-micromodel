This scenario represents the **Siege** archetype: a larger assault force must drive all the way into the defender’s spawn zone, while the defender is deliberately given a **forward contest objective** at midfield so it cannot simply turtle in place. I chose attackers with better mobility, broader firing arcs, and stronger sustained short-mid range pressure, while defenders have fewer but tougher ships with longer range and tighter firing cones. That creates a classic siege rhythm: the attackers want to survive the approach, fan around obstacles, and collapse isolated defenders; the defenders want to punish the advance before the assault mass can fully engage.

It should be useful for AI training because it forces several behaviors at once: coordinated advance under fire, lane selection through terrain, pressure on a fortified zone, and defender counter-movement away from pure spawn camping. The moderate obstacle field breaks up the map into partial firing lanes, so both factions must decide when to hold formation, when to flank, and when to commit through a gap. The stat balance keeps both sides dangerous without allowing instant deletions, producing a longer engagement with multiple decision points instead of a single opening volley deciding everything.
