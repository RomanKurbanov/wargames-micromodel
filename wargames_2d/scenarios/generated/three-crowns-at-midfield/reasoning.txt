This scenario is a **three-way free-for-all** built around a single central objective, with all three factions forced to converge on midfield instead of orbiting their own corners. I gave each faction a distinct battlefield identity while keeping overall combat power close: **Faction 0** is a fast raider group with good sustained fire and strong maneuverability, **Faction 1** is a compact armored force with the highest per-ship durability, and **Faction 2** is a disciplined long-range contingent that can punish exposed enemies from standoff distance. Their spawn triangle is wide enough to satisfy reaction-time safety rules, but close enough that the central zone becomes contested early.

This setup is useful for AI training because it creates unstable priorities and changing local advantages. Raiders can overextend into sniper fire, tanks can win direct trades but get surrounded, and long-range ships must reposition carefully when two enemies collapse on them. The central obstacle field encourages partial cover, lane selection, and flank timing rather than a simple straight-line brawl. Because all three factions share the same midfield objective, the battle teaches target switching, opportunistic third-party engagements, retreat decisions, and when to commit versus preserve formation.
