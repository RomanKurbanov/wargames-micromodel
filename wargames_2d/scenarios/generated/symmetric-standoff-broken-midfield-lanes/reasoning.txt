This scenario is a **symmetric standoff**: two equal fleets with mirrored stats begin on opposite sides of a moderately obstructed battlefield and push toward the center through broken firing lanes. I chose medium-speed, medium-range ships with enough durability to survive several volleys, so the fight is decided by formation integrity, angle management, and threat prioritization rather than by instant alpha strikes. The weapons have a limited firing arc and a reload cycle long enough to create visible attack windows, which encourages ships to reposition between bursts instead of simply orbiting and spraying continuously.

For training, this setup is useful because it isolates core tactical behaviors in a fair matchup: advancing into contact, using cover gaps, preserving spacing under collision-avoidance pressure, and deciding whether to chase exposed targets or hold a line. Since neither side has a raw statistical advantage, the outcome depends more on emergent micro-behavior than faction asymmetry, making it a strong baseline scenario for learning disciplined engagements.
