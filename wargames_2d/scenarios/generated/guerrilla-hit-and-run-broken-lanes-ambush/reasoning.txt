This scenario uses the **Guerrilla hit-and-run** archetype: a larger force of fast, fragile raiders fights a much smaller group of slow heavy ships. The raiders have high acceleration, strong turning, and a short reload cycle, letting them dart in, dump a burst, and disengage before the heavies can fully bring their guns to bear. The heavy faction trades mobility for durability, longer range, and punishing sustained presence; if the guerrillas mistime an approach or get caught in open lanes, the heavies can steadily grind them down.

The obstacle field is intentionally moderate and irregular so the AI must learn when to use cover, when to split around rocks, and when to collapse on isolated targets. This creates a useful training problem: the light faction should learn flanking, disengagement, and focus fire during reload windows, while the heavy faction should learn lane control, mutual support, and maintaining formation so raiders cannot peel ships away one by one. The result is a battle with repeated local skirmishes rather than one immediate line engagement.
